# Adaptation and final-confirmation decision

The first adaptation pruning retained `R1` and `F-free`. `F-generic` was
eliminated before final confirmation and its candidate file remains only as
immutable experimental ancestry.

The elimination criterion is operational, not stylistic: after calibration and
budget repair, `F-generic` still failed to complete two of four final private
adaptation cells under the common 200,000-token ceiling. Earlier public
mechanics calibration also measured a 170,849-token completion against a
160,000-token ceiling. Raising the ceiling again would tune the test to the
candidate and make cost unbounded.

`F-free` completed all four cells in that adaptation attempt. Because those
runs followed prompt and budget repairs, they were not eligible to decide
held-out admission. A final two-arm confirmation therefore froze `R1` and
`F-free` under the same assignments and common contract. Challenge rounds were
removed for every Council arm: generation one permits exactly two independent
first passes and one integration. Unresolved conflict is preserved as dissent
and an owner gate.

One byte-identical retry is allowed only for a transient execution or
structured-output transport failure. A semantic validation failure,
unauthorized claim, provenance failure, timeout, or budget overrun is never
retried.

In final confirmation, `R1` completed all four assignments and `F-free`
completed three. `F-free` produced no valid frozen output for the fourth within
the 200,000-token, 300-second contract. A later byte-identical diagnostic run
completed, but it cannot replace the failed frozen assignment.

The missing confirmation cell rejects `F-free` on reliability. Held-out was not
opened, so generation one rejects every Council runtime candidate without
making a comparative product-quality claim. No candidate bytes are eligible
for promotion.
